Where Water Damage Starts in Denver Properties

Starting at the top of a home and working toward the ground is a handy way to think about water damage. That is the route we take when inspecting a property in Denver, and five spots along it account for most calls.

Attics: The top of the house takes on water when shingles are damaged or the seal around a vent pipe gives out. Insulation acts like a sponge, so it can hold a surprising amount before any of it shows on the ceiling. Wet insulation stops insulating and keeps the wood beneath it damp. Our part is removing what is saturated and drying the wood, while a roofing contractor closes the opening.

Bathrooms: Bathroom floods usually come from a forgotten bathtub, a stopped-up sink or a toilet that will not quit rising. Even a tiled floor lets water pass at the grout, the tub edge and the base of the walls. When the bathroom is on an upper story, the water shows up as a wet patch overhead downstairs. Anything that came out of the toilet bowl should be treated as dirty water and cleaned with disinfectant.

Walls: Plumbing hidden in a wall may seep at a joint for weeks without leaving a puddle anywhere. The hints are small: a baseboard that swells, a stain that grows, or a patch of wall that feels cool and soft. On these calls in Denver, an infrared camera and a pin meter show us how far the dampness reaches. After the pipe is repaired by a plumber, we open just the damp part and dry the cavity.

Tanks: The hot water tank sits out of sight in a garage, closet or utility space and stays full all the time. Worn parts announce themselves with moisture at the top connections, the drain spout or the pressure relief pipe. When the tank itself fails, close the cold water valve above it and switch off the gas or power to the unit. Our Grand Junction Restoration crew extracts the water and checks the nearby walls and flooring, which often get wet from behind.

Drains: The lowest drain in the house is where a sewer blockage shows itself. Dark water rising in a floor drain or a ground-level shower means the main line is not carrying waste away. That water is Category 3, the most contaminated kind, and it takes protective equipment to clean up safely. Hold off on flushing or running taps, and keep the household clear while our team heads to Denver.

How Long Drying Takes in a Denver Home

A wet building left alone does dry eventually, but far too slowly. Moisture inside walls, studs and subfloors can linger for weeks, and that is plenty of time for mold to grow and wood to distort. Our job on every Denver property is to speed that up in a controlled way.

First the liquid water comes out, because extraction removes far more water per hour than evaporation ever will. Then air movers are set along the walls to push a fast stream of air across wet surfaces and lift moisture out of them. Dehumidifiers pull that moisture out of the air and send it down a drain, which keeps the room dry enough for evaporation to continue. Air movers on their own only stir humid air, so the two machines work as a pair.

We take moisture readings every day and compare them with a dry part of the same building. Most homes in Denver reach that dry standard in 3 to 5 days, with dense materials like hardwood or plaster taking longer. We remove the machines when the readings reach the goal, and not before.
