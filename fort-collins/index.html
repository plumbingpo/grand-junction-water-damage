Restoration Challenges Fort Collins Homeowners Face

Not every water problem announces itself with a flooded floor. Here is what homeowners around Fort Collins phone us about most, along with what usually sits behind each one.

Hidden leaks: A pinhole in a supply line or a loose drain fitting can drip inside a wall for months. Clues include flooring that feels spongy, blistered paint and a higher water bill you cannot explain. Our technicians trace the wet area with moisture meters and thermal cameras, without tearing out more wall than needed. Left alone, constant dampness rots framing and feeds mold, so a minor drip is still worth fixing now.

Sewage: When a main drain line clogs, wastewater comes back up through floor drains, tubs and toilets on the lowest level. It is typically caused by roots in the line, built-up grease or a municipal sewer overwhelmed during a downpour. Sewage counts as Category 3, the most contaminated class, and it is not safe to clean up bare-handed. Carpet, padding and other absorbent materials that got wet usually cannot be saved.

Storms: During a heavy storm, runoff may collect along the foundation faster than drains can carry it off. From there it comes in under doors, through window wells and across garage floors. Floodwater from outside picks up soil, fertilizer and whatever else it crossed, so we treat it as contaminated. A big enough storm can cause it at any time of year, and Fort Collins properties are no exception.

Crawlspace: The space beneath a raised floor is easy to forget, which lets dampness go unchecked for years. Standing water, a torn vapor barrier or a leaking pipe overhead keeps the air below your floors humid. Over time that leads to sagging insulation, musty odors indoors and soft or cupped flooring. Our team removes the water, dries the framing and can point out what is letting moisture in.

Condensate: Cooling equipment produces a steady stream of condensation whenever it runs in damp weather. That water should leave through a small drain line, which clogs easily with algae and dust. When it backs up, the pan overflows into the ceiling, closet or hallway around the air handler. Most owners learn of it from a ceiling stain or drooping drywall, so have the drain line cleared once a year.

Preventing Water Damage in Fort Collins Homes

A flood in the house feels like it came from nowhere, yet most trace back to wear that built up over months or years. For homeowners in Fort Collins, prevention is mainly a matter of knowing where to look.

Pipes and fittings top the list. Watch your water bill and your water pressure, because an unexplained rise in either can point to a hidden leak or stress on the pipes. Appliances are second. Do not run the washing machine or dishwasher when nobody is home, and replace the hoses on a schedule instead of waiting for one to fail. Third is the outside of the building. Rain gets in through damaged roofing, failed flashing, clogged gutters and ground that tilts toward the walls.

Leaving the house empty for a while? Close the main valve before you go, or have a neighbor look in. Homeowners in Fort Collins can ask Grand Junction Restoration about any of this. And if the worst happens anyway, a fast call keeps a small leak from turning into a large repair.
